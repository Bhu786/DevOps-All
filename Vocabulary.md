Yes. If your **main goal is fluency for DevOps + Java Full-Stack interviews**, I would not make you memorize 500 difficult words alone. That improves vocabulary, but **not speaking fluency enough**.

The best approach is:

### 🔥 Your daily formula

**1. Pronunciation — 10 min**
Take **10 difficult technical words** and say each 5 times.

**2. Technical sentences — 15 min**
Don't say only:

> “Idempotency.”

Say:

> “Idempotency means that performing the same operation multiple times produces the same result.”

**3. Interview answers — 20 min**
Pick **2 technical questions** and answer them aloud for 2–3 minutes each.

**4. Shadowing — 10 min**
Listen to good technical English and immediately repeat the speaker's sentence with the same rhythm.

**5. Spontaneous speaking — 10 min**
Pick one topic randomly:

> Kubernetes troubleshooting
> REST API versioning
> Kafka offset
> SSL/TLS
> CI/CD pipeline
> Microservices
> AWS architecture

Then explain it **without reading**.

### 🧠 Most important rule

Don't try to speak **difficult English**.

Try to speak **simple English with difficult technical vocabulary**.

For example:

❌ **Too complicated:**

> “The aforementioned architectural paradigm facilitates enhanced horizontal scalability through sophisticated orchestration mechanisms.”

✅ **Interview-quality:**

> “We use Kubernetes orchestration to achieve horizontal scalability. If traffic increases, the Horizontal Pod Autoscaler increases the number of pods.”

The second one sounds much more natural and professional.

### 🎯 Your target

Every day:

**10 words → 10 technical sentences → 2 interview answers → 10 minutes shadowing → 10 minutes spontaneous speaking**

Do this consistently for **30–60 days**, and you'll notice a major difference in how quickly you can formulate technical answers.

And for your particular goal, I'd focus on **speaking complete DevOps/Java interview answers**, not just pronunciation drills.
